\subsection*{I. Abstract (Maximum 100 Words)}
% ------------------------------------------------
\begin{enumerate}
    \item VisRAG ranks page images by cosine similarity and passes a fixed top-$k$ to the generator, but page weights use an unscaled softmax.
    \item At $\tau{=}1$ those weights are nearly uniform, so a fluent wrong page beats the true page and equal-width strips waste pixels.
    \item We fit $\tau$, marginalize matching answers, tile area by posterior mass, and stop once cumulative mass reaches $\rho$.
    \item In a seeded simulation matched to VisRAG's published retrieval statistics (3{,}607 queries), fitted $\tau$ cuts ECE from 0.575 to 0.094, and $k{=}5$ accuracy rises from 51.46\% to 68.34\%.
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{II. Proposed Novelty \& Technical Contribution}
% ------------------------------------------------
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Four selectors sit on the retriever's cosine scores: C1 marginalizes $P(a\mid q,d_j)$ with $p(d_j\mid q)$; C2 sets page area from $p_i^{\alpha}$; C3 keeps the shortest prefix with mass $\ge\rho$; S2 is a 97-parameter net on posterior, answer confidence, agreement, and rank.
    \item C1--C3 are training-free changes to decoding and page layout. S2 is trained with binary cross-entropy on the 20\% dev split, together with NLL-fitted $\tau$.
    \item VisRAG Eq.~(4) takes one page, $\arg\max_j P(a_j\mid q,d_j)\,\mathrm{softmax}(s)_j$, with equal strips and a fixed $k$. We replace that weight by $\mathrm{softmax}(s/\tau)$ and use it for the answer, the pixels, and $k$.
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{III. Theoretical / Mathematical Justification}
% ------------------------------------------------
\begin{enumerate}
    \item With cosine scores $s(q,d_j)$, $p(d_j\mid q)=\mathrm{softmax}(s/\tau)$. Pooling answers within the 5\% numeric rule,
    \[
        a^{\star}=\arg\max_a\sum_j P(a\mid q,d_j)\,p_j,\quad
        w_i=\frac{p_i^{\alpha}}{\sum_j p_j^{\alpha}},\quad
        k^{\star}=\min\{k\le k_{\max}:\textstyle\sum_{i\le k}p_i\ge\rho\}.
    \]
    \item $\tau=\exp(\log\tau)$ is fit by dev NLL. A lower page must beat a log-odds gap $(s_1-s_j)/\tau$, so in our simulation a fluent distractor ($\log P\approx-0.12$ to $-0.25$) loses to a moderate true page ($\log P\approx-0.35$ to $-0.55$). For $\alpha>0$, $p$ and $w$ are similarly ordered, so Chebyshev gives $\sum_i p_i w_i\ge 1/k$.
    \item One posterior therefore stops the flat product rule, raises true-page pixel share above $1/k$, and drops pages outside mass $\rho$.
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{IV. Comparative Results \& Empirical Evaluation}
% ------------------------------------------------
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data: a seeded simulation, not model runs.} For the six VisRAG sets (3{,}607 queries, Table~1 sizes), page ranks are sampled to match VisRAG-Ret's reported MRR@10 and Recall@10, generator log-probabilities are drawn from fixed ranges with set distractor rates, and latency is modeled at 180\,ms per page. Seed 0 uses 722 dev and 2{,}885 test queries; simulated test retrieval gives MRR@10 $=77.77\%$ and Recall@10 $=91.84\%$.
    \item Table~\ref{tab:results}: $k{=}5$ macro accuracy goes from 51.46\% ($\tau{=}1$) to 56.78\% ($\tau{=}0.02$), 64.92\% (Bayes), and 68.34\% (S2).
    \item ECE falls $0.575\to 0.229\to 0.094$ at fitted $\tau{=}0.0132$. Tiling ($\alpha{=}0.5$) beats equal-width concat at $k{=}2,3,6$; true-page pixel share is 0.550, 0.417, 0.317 versus $1/k$ (0.500, 0.333, 0.167). Adaptive $k^{\star}$ uses 3.08 pages and 554\,ms, against 56.09\% and 1{,}800\,ms at fixed $k{=}10$.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-6pt}
\textbf{Result Interpretation:}
At $\tau{=}1$, accuracy falls with $k$ (56.78, 56.02, 54.76, 51.46) because a fluent distractor enters a flat posterior. $\tau{=}0.02$ holds at 56.78. Bayes adds repeated answers ($+8.14$ at $k{=}5$); S2, which reads that agreement, reaches 68.34. Fixed $k{=}10$ is 6.96 points below $k{=}3$; $\rho{=}0.90$ stops at 3.08 pages and keeps 63.13. \textbf{Limitation:} distractor rates are simulation parameters, so these numbers show that each mechanism works when its assumptions hold, not how much it helps real VisRAG. Running VisRAG-Ret and MiniCPM-V on the real pages is the next step.
